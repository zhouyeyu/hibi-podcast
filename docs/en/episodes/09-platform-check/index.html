Are we on the right platform?

Welcome to Hibi, Japanese with Two Bears. Our bears are already inside a station. They have reached a platform, but one wants to check with her friend before waiting there. Today, ask one casual question to confirm that the place in front of you is the right one.

What are they checking?

Both bears are already looking at the platform. She has an idea, but wants her companion to confirm it. She is checking whether this place is right, rather than asking for directions from the beginning.

このホームで合ってる？

Her friend gives a positive answer. In this fictional conversation, this is the place they want. For a real journey, check your own ticket, train and station information. Our example does not identify any actual platform.

うん、ここで大丈夫。

She feels reassured and suggests waiting here. Follow the order: check, listen, then decide what to do. We are not learning train numbers or a complete travel conversation today.

One question for a companion

このホームで合ってる？

The meaning is, is this the right platform? The first part points to the platform in front of them. The rest checks whether that choice is correct. It is casual speech between companions. Do not treat it as the safest polite question for an unfamiliar staff member.

このホームで合ってる？

Imagine looking at the platform and then turning your eyes to your friend. Ask at an ordinary conversational pace. Leave room for the answer instead of stretching the ending or giving each sound a separate beat.

Change what you are checking

After leaving the station, you might check an exit. Replace only the item you are checking.

この出口で合ってる？

Is this the right exit? The purpose stays the same: ask your companion to confirm the place you are looking at.

Now imagine checking the place where you agreed to meet.

待ち合わせはここで合ってる？

Is this the right meeting place? The extra context tells your friend what you mean. You do not need a whole list of new place names today. Focus on the act of checking, then listening.
