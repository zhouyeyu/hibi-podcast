Welcome to Hibi, Japanese with Two Bears. Today, we find the station together and listen for a friendly suggestion to turn right. Let us listen to their conversation.

駅はどこかな？／あの角を右に曲がろう。

The scarf bear wonders where the station is. Her friend suggests turning right at that corner. The important idea is that they will move together. Today, we will practice one small suggestion and change its direction. We do not need a whole page of map vocabulary to decide what to do next.

駅はどこかな？

The first line means, I wonder where the station is. She shares a thought with the person beside her and invites them to think too. It has a reflective feeling. That is different from using a complete polite question to ask a stranger for directions. The relationship helps explain the tone.

あの角を右に曲がろう。

The reply means, let’s turn right at that corner. It gives a turning point, a direction, and a suggestion for shared action. The speaker is not ordering a passerby around. Nor does the sentence promise that the station is immediately after the corner. Follow only the route information that is actually there.

Our useful pattern is a suggestion to turn together. Listen to the short version, without the turning point at the beginning. The person making this suggestion is going along too. Keep that shared action in mind, instead of giving the line the tone of a command.

右に曲がろう。

The verb ending changes to express this let’s do it meaning. You can begin by learning the whole phrase for let’s turn right. Then change only the direction. The part that suggests acting together stays useful, so you do not have to rebuild the entire sentence from the beginning.

Left and right depend on which way the traveler is facing. A character on a screen can make that confusing. Imagine standing beside the bears and looking down the same road. Decide the direction first, then speak. Clear language is useful only if you have the right turn in mind.

左に曲がろう。

あの角を左に曲がろう。

The next version suggests turning left. Then we put that corner back into the sentence, to say where to turn. When there are several corners, that detail helps your friend. You can practice the short suggestion first, then add a location when the situation needs it.

Now listen once, then repeat during the gap. Keep the meaning in mind instead of chasing every change in pitch. There is time to finish each sentence. Start quietly if you like, then try again with a clear, comfortable voice.

駅はどこかな？

あの角を右に曲がろう。

右に曲がろう。

左に曲がろう。

あの角を左に曲がろう。

Now you are leading the way. You and your friend have reached the corner, and this time you need to turn left. Suggest that you turn together. If the longer sentence is difficult, start with the short version. Make the direction clear before worrying about adding the turning point.

右？左？

左に曲がろう。

The model answer suggests turning left. That was our practice route; the original conversation still says right. Keep those two directions separate. Listen again to the original and notice how the bears move from wondering about the station to suggesting one small action they can take together.
