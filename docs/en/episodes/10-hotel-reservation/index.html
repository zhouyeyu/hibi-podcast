You already have a reservation

Welcome to Hibi, Japanese with Two Bears. You arrive at a hotel with your luggage. The room is already booked. What can you say first at the front desk? Today, clearly explain that you have a reservation, without memorizing a complete check-in conversation.

Listen at the front desk

すみません、予約しています。

はい、確認いたします。

ありがとうございます。

Your turn · 2 seconds

The scarf-wearing bear is the guest. Her friend in blue is working at the desk. She politely gets his attention and says she has a reservation. He says he will check, and she thanks him. Listen again to the statement we need today.

予約しています。

State what is already arranged

Her booking already exists. She is explaining that fact, rather than asking to make a new booking or cancelling one. The staff member will still need to check details. Today, we are practicing only this first small step.

すみません、予約しています。

The reply means the staff member will check. It does not mean every check-in step is complete. Leave space for the information that still needs to be confirmed.

はい、確認いたします。

A short thank you lets the other person continue. This exchange can be brief and still be clear. We do not invent the bears’ names, identity documents or booking records. Focus on making the purpose of the visit clear.

ありがとうございます。

One useful statement

予約しています。

The meaning is, I have a reservation. At the desk, the situation makes the subject clear. You do not need to name every booking detail in this opening sentence. Learn it as one polite statement rather than studying a table of verb forms today.

予約しています。

Speak at a normal pace and finish clearly. This is a statement, so you do not need to turn it into a long, uncertain question. A polite opening can come first when you want the staff member’s attention.

Add one piece of information

If your reservation is for two people, add that detail first.

二人で予約しています。

This version explains the number of people. Always match such details to your actual booking. Learning a sentence does not mean any number will fit your situation.

Now move the same idea to a restaurant. Imagine you booked a table for seven in the evening.

七時に予約しています。

This adds the reserved time. Seven is only our practice situation, rather than a hotel check-in rule. You are still describing an existing reservation, not requesting a new one.

Listen, then state your purpose

Your turn. Listen to the whole sentence, then repeat during the pause.

予約しています。

Your turn · 3 seconds

Again, as if you had just reached the desk.

予約しています。

Your turn · 3 seconds

Include the polite opening this time.

すみません、予約しています。

Your turn · 4 seconds

Add that your reservation is for two people.

二人で予約しています。

Your turn · 4 seconds

At a restaurant, say that your reservation is for seven.

七時に予約しています。

Your turn · 4 seconds

Speak from the situation

You reach the hotel with your luggage. You booked the room earlier. A staff member looks up from the front desk. Explain your situation now, without reading the words.

Your turn · 5 seconds

予約しています。

Adding the polite opening also works. First establish that a reservation exists. Then let the staff member ask for the details they need, rather than giving everything at once.

Try a restaurant next. You and a friend have booked for two people. Explain that your reservation is for two.

Your turn · 5 seconds

二人で予約しています。

There is no need to imitate the voice perfectly. Check that you are describing an existing booking, rather than asking to make one now. Once the purpose is clear, work on a natural pause.

Listen again and take it with you

Return to the hotel desk. Listen for the guest’s explanation and the staff member’s response, without translating each sound.

すみません、予約しています。

はい、確認いたします。

ありがとうございます。

Your turn · 2 seconds

Here is today’s word. You may hear it at a hotel, a restaurant, or another place where reservations are made.

予約
