Welcome to Hibi, Japanese with Two Bears. Today, we choose a rice ball and listen for how to make a thoughtful choice. Let us listen to their conversation.

おにぎり、どれにする？／鮭にしようかな。／じゃあ、私も！

He chooses the salmon filling, and the scarf bear decides to have the same one. Let us return to the first question and hear how they reach that decision.

おにぎり、どれにする？

The question means, which rice ball will you have? There are several choices in front of them. The friends can see the options, so they can keep the question short. Imagine pointing toward that shelf as you listen.

鮭にしようかな。

He is thinking aloud: I think I’ll have the salmon one. The decision is leaning one way, but the tone leaves a little room for thought. It is more reflective than a firm announcement.

じゃあ、私も！

Her reply means, then I’ll have that too. She does not repeat the filling or the whole choosing phrase, because both friends already know what they are discussing. Shared context makes the short response clear.

Our useful idea today is choosing something. First, listen to a version that sounds more settled. Picture yourself having made up your mind, rather than still comparing the options.

これにする。

That means, I’ll have this one. Put the chosen item first, then use the choosing part of the sentence. You can make a clear decision without naming every option on the shelf.

鮭にしようかな。

Now compare the original line. The speaker is leaning toward a choice while still considering it. A natural English meaning is, I think I’ll have this one. Keep that thoughtful feeling in mind instead of treating the line as a rigid announcement.

Today, hear the difference between a settled choice and a choice you are considering. We will practice the more thoughtful version, then move it into another familiar situation.

Now walk over to the drinks. The same friend asks which one you will have. You want tea this time. Keep the choosing expression and replace the salmon filling with tea. Try it before hearing the model.

お茶にしようかな。

That means, I think I’ll have tea. The choice changes, while the rest stays familiar. Now imagine that you would prefer coffee.

コーヒーにしようかな。

This time the choice is coffee. The same expression works beyond the rice-ball shelf. Start with familiar drinks and make one pattern useful, rather than collecting lots of new words at once.

Listen, then repeat during the gap. Imagine you really are beside the shelf with a friend. Begin with the question about what they will choose. Keep a comfortable pace and leave yourself time to finish.

おにぎり、どれにする？

Next, answer while considering your choice. Keep the voice relaxed, as though you are deciding, rather than reciting a test answer.

鮭にしようかな。

The last reply agrees with the friend’s choice. You have heard what they want, and you would like the same thing.

じゃあ、私も！

Try one more question and answer. Choose tea or coffee for yourself. Keep your own choice in mind when you speak.

どれにする？

Now take away the hint. You are at the drinks cabinet, with tea and coffee available. Your friend asks which one you will have. Say that you think you will have tea.

お茶にしようかな。

Coffee would also be a valid choice. The important thing is using the pattern to express your own decision. Can you remember today’s food word? Picture the rice ball.

おにぎり。

Listen to the original again. See whether the question, the thoughtful choice, and the agreement are easier to follow now.

おにぎり、どれにする？／鮭にしようかな。／じゃあ、私も！

Take a natural way to make your own choice with you. Try using it in your own day. This is Hibi, Japanese with Two Bears. See you in the next everyday moment.
