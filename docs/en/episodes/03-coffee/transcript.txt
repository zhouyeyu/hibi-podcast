You have been walking for a while, and you catch the smell of coffee. You might turn to a friend and say that you feel like having some. Our bears are having that kind of conversation. They are not ordering at the counter yet. Listen for the wish, then for the friend’s helpful response.

コーヒー、飲みたいな。／あそこにカフェがあるよ。

The scarf bear wants coffee. The blue-shirt bear points out a cafe over there. One person shares a wish, and the other offers a possible place to go. Nobody has ordered a drink or chosen a cup size. Keep that friendly conversation separate from the different task of asking a server for something.

コーヒー、飲みたいな。

Her first line means, I feel like having coffee. She is describing her own wish, rather than insisting that her friend buy it. Because they know each other, the line is short and relaxed. In casual conversation, some connecting words can be left out when the meaning is already clear.

あそこにカフェがあるよ。

The answer means, there is a cafe over there. Her friend responds to the wish by pointing out a place they could go. Over there refers to somewhere away from both of them. Imagine both bears looking toward the cafe, and the two lines begin to feel like one connected moment.

Our pattern today expresses wanting to drink something. Listen to a simple version. It describes a wish: not a drink you already have, and not a direct order to a server. Those situations can involve the same drink, but the speaker is doing a different job in each one.

コーヒーを飲みたい。

The Japanese verb changes form when we express wanting to do the action. For now, learn want to drink as a useful chunk. You do not need a complete verb chart today. If you can keep that chunk and change coffee to tea, you are already using the pattern for yourself.

The original line also has a gentle, reflective ending, rather like thinking aloud. It is not something you must add to every sentence. Do not turn the practice into exaggerated acting. Keep a real wish in mind, and let the sentence sound like something you would naturally say to a friend.

お茶を飲みたい。

水を飲みたい。

Now change coffee to tea, then water. The wish stays the same, while the drink changes. Imagine a break in a tea shop, then a thirsty moment after a walk. Stay with one action and a few familiar drinks before trying to learn lots of other verbs.
