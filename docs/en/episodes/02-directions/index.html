You are at an unfamiliar street corner, trying to find the station. Your map is not helping, so you decide to ask someone nearby. Listen to the bears and follow two pieces of information: what the scarf bear is looking for, and what direction she is given. Picture the road in front of you.

She politely asks where the station is. The blue-shirt bear tells her to go straight along this road, and she thanks him. That gives us a simple sequence: get someone’s attention, ask about a place, listen to the directions, then respond. Let us hear how the individual lines do those jobs.

すみません、駅はどこですか？

The opening politely gets the other person’s attention. Then she asks where the station is. She is asking about its location, not asking what a station means. This complete question is useful when speaking to someone you do not know. Ask clearly, then leave room for the person to answer.

この道をまっすぐ行ってください。

The reply means, please go straight along this road. The speaker identifies the road, then tells her how to continue. Imagine moving forward along it. The sentence does not tell us how many minutes to walk, or whether the station is on the right. Listen to what is actually given.

ありがとうございます。

Her final line is a thank-you. It acknowledges the help she has received. If you do not understand a direction, you can ask for clarification before finishing the conversation. You do not have to pretend everything is clear just because you have reached the last line of a practice dialogue.

Our one useful question today is, where is a particular place? Listen to a shorter version without the opening attention-getter. The place comes first, followed by the question about its location. Hear it as a whole question before trying to change any part.

That question is about the station. We can change the place while keeping the location question. The same structure can help you find a restroom or a convenience store. You are not learning three unrelated sentences; you are finding one familiar piece inside three useful situations.

Being polite also means giving the listener time. You do not need to repeat the question several times without a pause. Decide what you are looking for, ask once clearly, and listen. Today, one useful question matters more than memorizing a whole list of direction words.
