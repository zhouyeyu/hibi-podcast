You are at a small tea shop. Something on the counter looks good, and you want to buy it. How do you move from asking the price to making a simple request? Listen to our two bears. Your first listening goal is small: find out how many the scarf bear asks for.

She checks the price, hears one thousand yen, and asks for one. The exchange has a useful order: ask, listen, then request. You do not need to understand every sound at once. Let us replay the lines and connect each one to what is happening at the counter.

すみません、このお茶はいくらですか？

The opening gets the shopkeeper’s attention. It does not necessarily mean that the customer has done anything wrong. She then asks how much this tea costs. The item is right there, so both people know what she means. Pointing at a real product helps make a short question clear.

はい、千円です。

The answer is one thousand yen. Listen for the amount before deciding whether to buy. If you miss it, you can ask to hear it again. For this scene, we only need to understand that the price has been given, so the customer can now make her decision.

じゃあ、一つください。

Her last line means, then, one please. The opening connects her request to the price she has just heard. She does not repeat the name of the tea because it is already understood. A short request can be complete when the other person knows exactly which item you mean.

Our useful pattern today is asking for an item. Listen to a version that names the tea. The ending makes a polite request to the person serving you. We are asking for something, rather than simply describing what we like.

お茶をください。

That means, tea please. Put the item first, then use the same request ending. If the item is already clear, you can instead give the quantity, as the scarf bear did. Listen for the whole request as one useful piece of language.

What counts as one item depends on what the shop is selling. It might be a packet offered as a single unit. Do not treat this quantity word as the only way to count every cup, bottle, or box. For now, point clearly and make one simple request.

コーヒーをください。

これを一つください。
