She asks to borrow the umbrella, hears yes, and says thanks. Does the opening announce that she is taking it, or ask whether that would be okay? Listen to the question again.

傘、借りてもいい？

Both friends can see the umbrella, so there is no need for a long introduction. The first part names the thing she wants to borrow. The rest makes it a question and leaves the decision with her friend.

傘、借りてもいい？

The reply means yes, go ahead. In this exchange, her friend has clearly agreed. Notice that she asks first and listens for the answer. Learning to borrow something includes that small moment of waiting.

うん、どうぞ。

She closes with a simple thank-you. The language is relaxed because these two know each other. Follow the purpose of each line before trying to translate every sound separately.

ありがとう。

傘、借りてもいい？

This means, can I borrow the umbrella? Name the item, then keep the question together. Finish clearly and give your friend time to respond. You are making a request rather than giving an instruction.

傘、借りてもいい？

Practice this relaxed version with familiar friends. A conversation with someone you do not know may call for more polite language. For today, stay with the relationship in our scene, and keep the ending light instead of drawing it out.

You need to write something, but have no pen. Ask a friend using the same borrowing question.

ペン、借りてもいい？

Can I borrow the pen? The item changes, while the purpose remains the same. Name the pen and finish the question without rushing.

Now imagine an empty place beside your friend. Ask if you can sit there.

ここに座ってもいい？

Can I sit here? This asks about an action rather than borrowing an object. Learn it as one complete line linked to the empty seat. You do not need a list of every possible action today.
